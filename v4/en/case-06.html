M&A Roll-Up Strategy for a Civil Engineering IT Company

We supported a civil engineering IT company that was pursuing step-change growth through M&A but had no system in place to continuously identify candidate companies. By designing a roll-up strategy and building an in-house sourcing foundation, we enabled the company to make investment decisions quickly and accurately.

CHALLENGEChallenge

The company had considered business expansion through M&A as a strategic option, but lacked the foundation to put it into practice. Investment criteria, such as how to select acquisition targets and which areas to build up first, had not been codified. In addition, the company had no in-house system for identifying candidate companies, forcing an ad hoc response every time a deal arose. As a result, M&A had not yet become a functioning, continuous growth driver.

APPROACHApproach

We first formulated a roll-up strategy grounded in the company's business characteristics and market environment, organizing the direction and priority of acquisitions, namely which areas and functions to bring in first. Next, to ensure the strategy did not remain merely theoretical, we built an in-house sourcing foundation capable of continuously identifying candidate companies. The aim was to shift from a structure dependent on external intermediaries to one in which the company keeps finding candidate companies itself. We advanced strategy formulation and the building of the execution foundation together.

IMPACTImpact

With the roll-up strategy now codified, acquisition priorities and investment criteria are beginning to function as a shared language across management. Building the in-house sourcing foundation has enabled the company to identify candidate companies on its own on an ongoing basis. This has improved both the speed and accuracy of M&A investment decisions. A structure for finding candidate companies without relying on external intermediaries will support future step-change growth.
